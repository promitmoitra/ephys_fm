What we found

A filter-bank covariance fingerprint solves person ID here. Per-band spatial covariance → Riemannian tangent space → logistic regression identifies the 21 Dreyer evaluation people from one 4-s window at 0.996 on the hidden runs (pre-registered confirmation). Soft routing then reaches the oracle-ID ceiling, so on Dreyer there is nothing left for a better fingerprint to gain.

Why: identity lives in the spatial structure of alpha/beta oscillations. The 8–13 Hz band alone identifies BNCI people across days at 0.975; delta (1–4 Hz) is just as good within a session but day-specific (0.710 across days). A broadband covariance is dominated by that delta power, which is why it failed across days (0.872).

The EEGNet fingerprint was weaker than it looked: at a fixed 150-epoch budget (no epoch picked on R3), it scores 0.618 on R3 with NLL 2.26. The shipped 0.857 came from selecting the epoch on R3 itself.

Shippable: zero-phase filtering on a fixed window is one T × T matrix per band. The whole fingerprint runs in pure torch and matches the sklearn pipeline to 4e-6 (5.7 MB).
